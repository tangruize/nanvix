### 3. Remaining semantic differences?

**None found.** Detailed analysis:

- **`run()` operation order:** Original calls `context_mut()` → `take_interrupt_reason()` →
  `get_thread_data_area()`. Verified omits `context_mut()` and preserves the remaining order.
  `context_mut()` is a pure pointer extraction with no side effects on interrupt_reason or TDA,
  so omitting it does not alter the semantics of the remaining operations. ✓

- **`run()` mutability:** Original uses `mut self` (needed for `context_mut()`). Verified uses
  `self` with `let mut state = self.state` (move + rebind as mutable). Since Verus moves
  ownership of `self.state` into `state`, the `&mut` calls on `state` are valid. Semantically
  equivalent. ✓

- **`terminate()` exit status:** The conversion chain `ErrorCode::Interrupted` (#[repr(i32)],
  EINTR=4 per `errno.rs:21`) → `From<ErrorCode> for ExitStatus` (`Self(error.into())` =
  `ExitStatus(4u32)`) is correctly modeled as `EXIT_STATUS_INTERRUPTED() = 4`. ✓

- **Boundary models (`RunningThread`, `ZombieThread`, `RunResult`):** These are structural
  wrappers with postconditions documenting cross-module verification obligations. They do not
  alter exec semantics. ✓

- **Invented functions:** `set_interrupt_reason`, `store_mutex_guard`, `take_mutex_guard` are
  direct forwarding to `self.state.method()`, equivalent to `self.thread_state_mut().method()`.
  They add no new logic, only verified contracts. ✓
